🧭 Design: one Short = one new term → Skool

The content design behind every Short in this series, and the flywheel that is supposed to make each one help the next. It is a hypothesis to test, not a proven result.

💡 The idea

1 · Give people a new term

Every Short teaches one term people need to learn (this one: LSP). A term is a handle: once you have the word you can search it, ask about it and use it.

2 · Make it matter now

A fun metaphor and one concrete payoff (LSP = “glasses” for a cheap model, so you don't pay $200). The Short earns attention; the term earns curiosity.

3 · Send them to Skool to learn it

The Short can't teach setup in 45 s. The pinned comment explains the term in 3 sentences and points to Delivery Pilot on Skool for the step-by-step, the Q&A and the people.

How it spins

  1. Short → term. Each Short ends with the viewer holding one new word. Cost: one idea per video, no feature dumps.
  2. Curiosity → pinned comment. “What is LSP?” is answered in 3 sentences right where they are asking, with the link. No click needed to get the value; a click is needed to get depth.
  3. Skool → learning. The community holds what a Short cannot: setup steps, checks (“is it really passing diagnostics?”), people to ask. Each term gets a lesson and a thread.
  4. Members try it → wins and stuck points. Screenshots of an agent fixing a diagnostic are proof; stuck points are the next lesson.
  5. Proof + questions → next term. The questions in Skool and in YouTube comments are the topic list. Proof clips become b-roll for the next Short (fixes the “no proof on screen” gap).
  6. Next term → next Short. Each new Short links back to earlier terms, so every Short is also an entry point to the whole library.

Why it should compound (three loops)

📺 Content loop

Questions from members → topics → Shorts. Topic research gets cheaper and more relevant with every cycle.

🏫 Community loop

More members → more wins and answers → a better reason for the next viewer to join.

📚 Library loop

Every term lesson stays. New Shorts point to old terms; old lessons keep working for new viewers.
